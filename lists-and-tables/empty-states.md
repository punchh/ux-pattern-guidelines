# Empty states

{% hint style="success" icon="sparkles" %}
[**Get the AI skill**](empty-states.md#ai-skill-file) for this UX pattern guideline in a markdown (.MD) file.
{% endhint %}

"Empty state" and "blank state" are sometimes used interchangeably - but they shouldn't be. There's important nuance and experience distinction between the 2.

And neglects to mention the 3rd variation: The "No Results" state.

### Empty states

In an empty state, the user has completed or cleared a task(s), there are no items to show, there’s nothing to do about it, and that’s okay (or may even be a good thing).  

Think of a to-do list, or email inbox.

#### Happy empty state

The current user personally accomplished something, or personally benefits because of the empty state.

You'll want to write these using words that are relevant to the content or business context for whatever feature you're working on, but as a casual generic example:

**Main text:**

> There's nothing left to do

**Text underneath main text:**

> Give yourself a pat on the back

#### Matter-of-fact empty state

The list is empty because of circumstances outside of the current user’s control (but it’s not a bad thing - just a matter of fact).

You'll want to write these using words that are relevant to the content or business context for whatever feature you're working on, but as a casual example:

**Main text:**

> No gift cards to see

(Where "gift cards" is replaced by whatever the content type is for your situation)

**Text underneath main text:**

> The guest hasn't added any gift cards yet

(Where "guest" is replaced by the actor for your context, and "gift cards" is replaced by the content type for your situation)

### Blank states

The current user hasn’t populated any items here yet. Having some items would be a good thing, and we want to encourage it.

You'll want to write these using words that are relevant to the content or business context for whatever feature you're working on, but as a casual example:

**Main text:**

> No campaigns yet

(Where "campaigns" is replaced by whatever the content type is for your situation)

**Text underneath main text:**

> Start building guest loyalty by creating a campaign

(Replace "start building guest loyalty" with whatever incentive is relevant for your context, and campaign is replaced by whatever content type is for your situation)

**Button**

To encourage participation, we sometimes include a primary CTA button at the bottom of the empty state, inviting the user to create the content.

> Create a campaign

(Replace "campaign" with whatever content is relevant for your scenario)

<figure><img src="../.gitbook/assets/image (106).png" alt=""><figcaption></figcaption></figure>

***

### No results states

The user has performed a search or filter configuration that doesn’t match any items. Tell them what can help and let them do it quickly.

The text here can usually be pretty generic and still effective, so we suggest:

**Main text:**

> No matches found

**Text underneath main text:**

> Try pairing down your filter

**Button**

To shortcut clearing any filters in one fell swoop, offer the user a button at the bottom of the empty state to clear filters.

> Clear filters

<figure><img src="../.gitbook/assets/image (107).png" alt=""><figcaption></figcaption></figure>

***

### "Coming soon" states

The feature isn’t available yet under any user controlled condition.

The product team / organization hasn’t released it to anyone yet.  It can also be accompanied with a sneak peak of what’s to come. The design goal is to generate excitement and manage expectations.

You'll want to write these using words that are relevant to the content or business context for whatever feature you're working on, but as a casual example:

**Main text:**

> Gifting options are coming soon

(Where "gifting options" is replaced by whatever the content type is for your situation)

**Text underneath main text:**

> Brace for sending offers and rewards to friends and family. Expected Q3 2028

(Where the text is replaced by whatever benefit or business value your users will get when the feature is released)

**Button**

Get users engaged in following your product by including a primary CTA button at the bottom of the empty state, inviting the user to sign up for updates:

> Notify me

<figure><img src="../.gitbook/assets/image (109).png" alt=""><figcaption></figcaption></figure>

#### Key elements and messages in a "coming soon" state:

✅ Articulate the benefit

✅ Provide an estimated date or time range when it will realistically be available (inspire hope)

✅ Offer a notification sign-up
